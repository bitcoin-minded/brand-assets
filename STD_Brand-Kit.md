# Bitcoin-Minded — Brand Kit

This document defines the visual identity of Bitcoin-Minded — color
palette, typography, logo system, and the per-course imprint
framework. Every visual surface produced under this brand — course
videos, slides, course landing pages, marketing materials, email,
social — should follow the rules below.

A consistent visual identity does the same work that consistent voice
does: it builds trust over time. A reader who sees a Bitcoin-Minded
slide deck, then a course landing page, then an email, then a printed
handout should experience them as one company even if the surfaces are
completely different. That coherence is what this document protects.

## Brand Direction

The visual identity is calibrated to the audience defined in
[[STD_Ideal-Customer-Profiles]] — adults aged 35-65 who distrust
hype, financial pressure tactics, and the visual register of 2020s
crypto and Web3 brands. The identity reads as a serious independent
publisher of practitioner-level material, in the tier of independent
courses by Tim Ferriss, Tiago Forte, or David Perell. Warm paper
backgrounds, near-black ink type, a single deliberate non-Bitcoin-orange
accent, and three subtle per-course imprint colors. Display typography
is a serif (Fraunces). Body typography is a humanist sans (DM Sans).

The identity is deliberately not:

- Bitcoin maximalist (no orange-on-black, no laser eyes, no rocket
  imagery)
- Web3 / SaaS startup (no Tailwind teal, no electric purple, no
  neon-on-dark, no abstract crypto art)
- Financial services corporate (no glass-and-steel skyscrapers, no
  navy-and-gold, no stock chart imagery)
- Tech bro (no oversized geometric sans wordmarks, no aggressive
  capitalization, no gradient backgrounds)

These exclusions are as load-bearing as the inclusions. The identity
works because it sits visibly outside those categories.

## Master Palette

The master palette is shared across all courses and applies to every
non-course-specific surface.

| Name        | Hex       | RGB           | Role                                                                                                                                     |
| ----------- | --------- | ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Paper       | `#F4EFE6` | 244, 239, 230 | Default background — warm off-white that reads as paper rather than screen-white. Used for 70-80% of any surface area.                   |
| Paper warm  | `#EBE3D4` | 235, 227, 212 | Card backgrounds, closing CTA panels, surfaces that need to differentiate from primary paper without leaving the warm range.             |
| Ink         | `#1A1714` | 26, 23, 20    | Body text, headings, primary type. Warm near-black, never pure black.                                                                    |
| Ink mute    | `#5A4F45` | 90, 79, 69    | Secondary text — captions, meta, footer copy. Same hue as Ink, lower contrast.                                                           |
| Rule        | `#D8CDB8` | 216, 205, 184 | Hairline rules, subtle dividers, table borders.                                                                                          |
| Rule strong | `#BFB39A` | 191, 179, 154 | Stronger dividers where additional weight is needed.                                                                                     |
| Accent      | `#B85C2A` | 184, 92, 42   | Master accent — desaturated terracotta. Used for italic emphasis words in headlines, hero CTAs on hover, the quiet two-tone emblem mark. |

The master accent is deliberately not Bitcoin orange (`#F7931A`).
Bitcoin orange signals the maximalist aesthetic this brand sits
outside of. Terracotta sits in the same warm family but reads as
considered editorial color, not category signaling.

## Imprint Colors

Each of the three courses (BFP, BFB, BFA) has a dedicated imprint
color that signals course identity without dominating the master
palette. Imprint colors appear in small quantities only — as eyebrow
text, italic accent words inside headlines, section number labels,
left-edge stripes on curriculum rows, hairline rules above the
trust meta-row, and as a band along the top of the closing CTA.
Imprint colors never tint backgrounds, button fills, or body type.

| Course | Name        | Hex       | RGB          |
| ------ | ----------- | --------- | ------------ |
| BFP    | Honey ochre | `#8C6A2F` | 140, 106, 47 |
| BFB    | Slate teal  | `#2D4A47` | 45, 74, 71   |
| BFA    | Ink navy    | `#1F2748` | 31, 39, 72   |

Each imprint color is calibrated against a specific common alternative
that the brand deliberately avoids:

- BFP honey ochre is not Bitcoin orange (`#F7931A`). The reasoning is
  the same as for the master accent — Bitcoin orange carries category
  signaling that the BFP audience reads as hype.
- BFB slate teal is not the Tailwind / Web3 default teal (`#0F766E`).
  Tailwind teal reads as 2020s SaaS startup. Slate teal is darker,
  dustier, closer to British racing green or aged brass — owner-class
  rather than founder-class.
- BFA ink navy is not corporate finance navy (`#003366`). Corporate
  navy performs financial-services authority. Ink navy is the color
  of a financial journal cover or quarterly report binding —
  restrained enough to sit next to fiduciary copy without performing
  finance.

## Color Usage Rules

### Proportional dominance

On any given surface, paper and ink dominate. The master accent
appears in trace quantity. Imprint colors appear in trace quantity.
The proportions to aim for on a typical page or slide:

- Paper / paper warm backgrounds — 70 to 80 percent of the surface
- Ink (body type, headings) — 15 to 25 percent
- Master accent — under 5 percent
- Imprint color — under 5 percent on per-course surfaces, zero on
  shared surfaces

### Colors that must never be used

These specific colors are excluded from all Bitcoin-Minded surfaces.
They carry category signals the brand sits outside of, and using them
even in small amounts undermines the broader visual identity.

- Bitcoin orange `#F7931A` and any saturated orange in that range
- Pure black `#000000` (Ink `#1A1714` is used instead — warm near-black)
- Pure white `#FFFFFF` (Paper `#F4EFE6` is used instead — warm off-white)
- Tailwind teal `#0F766E` and similar SaaS-default teals
- Electric purple, neon green, or any high-saturation digital-native
  accent color
- Corporate finance navy `#003366` and similar high-trust-bank navies

### Print considerations

The hex values above are the master reference. For print contexts that
require CMYK, convert via Pantone Bridge or the print vendor's preferred
conversion. The warm off-white paper color and warm near-black ink
require careful conversion — do not let an inexperienced print operator
substitute pure white and pure black on the assumption that they are
"close enough." The warmth is load-bearing.

## Typography

The brand uses three typefaces:

- **Fraunces** for all display type — headlines, page titles, pull
  quotes, the wordmark.
- **DM Sans** for all body type — paragraphs, navigation, buttons,
  form labels, captions.
- **JetBrains Mono** for trace meta only — lesson counts, hex codes,
  section numbers, the small URL inside browser-bar mockups. Never
  for body content.

All three are open-source and free for commercial use. All three are
available on Google Fonts and as desktop-installable font files for
Pages, Keynote, PowerPoint, Word, and similar applications.

### Portable type rules

Typography rules are deliberately portable. They use only properties
that exist in every consumer text app — font family, weight, size,
italic toggle, line-height. They do not use font-variation-settings,
fractional letter-spacing values, or per-element optical-size tuning.
A correctly-set headline in Pages will match a correctly-set headline
on the website, because the rule is just "Fraunces, weight 500,
italic on emphasis words."

The rules:

- **Fraunces (display)** — weight 400 or 500. Italic for emphasis
  words inside headlines. Default tracking. Use at any size from
  approximately 18px to 80px. Do not use weights below 400 or above
  500. Do not adjust letter-spacing.
- **DM Sans (body)** — weight 400 for body copy, 500 or 600 for
  emphasis and buttons, 700 for strong emphasis. Body sizes 15 to
  17px with line-height 1.55 to 1.65. Maximum line length
  approximately 68 characters.
- **JetBrains Mono (trace meta)** — weight 400 or 500. Use only at
  small sizes (11 to 12px) for short labels. Never use for body or
  paragraph content.

### Hierarchy

A typical page hierarchy uses:

| Level | Family | Weight | Approx. size |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 (page title) | Fraunces | 500 | 40-58px |
| H2 (section title) | Fraunces | 400 or 500 | 28-40px |
| H3 (sub-section) | Fraunces | 500 | 22-28px |
| Body | DM Sans | 400 | 15-17px |
| Eyebrow / meta | DM Sans | 500 (uppercase, letter-spacing 0.14em) | 12-13px |
| Trace meta | JetBrains Mono | 400 | 11-12px |

Uppercase eyebrow text is the one place where letter-spacing is part
of the typographic rule (positive `0.14em` for readability of small
caps). All other letter-spacing is left at default.

### Italic for emphasis

Inside headlines, italic Fraunces is used for emphasis words — typically
one to three words per headline. Italic words on a course page take
the imprint color; italic words on master-palette surfaces take the
accent color (terracotta). Italics are reserved for headlines and
pull quotes — never used inside body copy for emphasis.

### What never to do with type

- Never substitute Fraunces with another serif (Tiempos, GT Sectra,
  Merriweather, Times) for budget or availability reasons. The brand
  is the type as much as it is the color.
- Never substitute DM Sans with Inter. Inter is the post-2020 product
  default and reads as the SaaS register the brand sits outside of.
- Never apply font-variation-settings, custom axis values, or
  fractional letter-spacing to live type outside the logo files.
  Those settings cannot be reproduced in Pages, Keynote, or most
  consumer applications.
- Never set body copy in Fraunces. Fraunces is display only.
- Never set body copy in JetBrains Mono. Mono is trace meta only.

## Wordmark Fine-Tuning (Logo-Only)

The wordmark in the logo lockup files uses Fraunces with the variable
axes configured for a specific visual register: weight 500, opsz 144,
SOFT 50, WONK 0, letter-spacing −0.022em. SOFT 50 partially rounds
the letter terminals, which bridges the geometric emblem and the
otherwise sharp typographic system.

These settings are baked into the logo SVG files as flattened vector
outlines. The letterforms are converted to permanent paths during
export from Figma — the wordmark is no longer live type, it is a
vector image. As a result, the fine-tuning carries everywhere the
SVG goes, regardless of whether Fraunces is installed in the
rendering environment.

The fine-tuning lives in the logo files and only there. Live type
elsewhere in the brand uses the simpler portable rules above. This
distinction is intentional: the wordmark gets refinement that the
rest of the system cannot guarantee, because the wordmark is a
single permanent asset rather than text that gets re-rendered.

## Logo System

The logo system has two asset types:

- **Lockups** — the emblem paired with the Bitcoin-Minded wordmark.
  Used in nav bars, page footers, business cards, slide decks,
  document headers, and any context wide enough to display both
  the mark and the company name legibly.
- **Emblems** — the mark alone. Used in contexts smaller than
  approximately 80 pixels wide where the wordmark would become
  illegible — favicons, app icons, social profile pictures, video
  corner watermarks, avatars in user lists.

A lockup is the default. An emblem is used only when space requires it.

### Lockup variants

Eleven lockup variants exist. Each is a separate SVG file in
`/Branding/Logos/`. Use the variant that matches the surface.

| Variant | When to use |
|---|---|
| Primary positive | Default. Use on any paper or paper-warm background. |
| Primary negative | Use on dark backgrounds — ink, black photography, dark video frames. |
| Primary accent | Use when a single high-impact brand surface is needed — a hero block, a marketing campaign opener, a poster. The terracotta surface is meant to stand out, not blend in. |
| Primary quiet two-tone | Soft variant for editorial contexts where the lockup should be present but not dominant — body content with a logo aside, a subtle nav, an email signature. Terracotta emblem with ink wordmark on transparent. |
| Monochrome ink | Universal fallback. Use when only one color is permissible — fax, embossing, single-color print, simple badge. |
| BFP imprint | Use on BFP-specific surfaces where the lockup carries course identity — BFP landing page hero, BFP cohort welcome, BFP printed handout. Paper on honey ochre. |
| BFP quiet two-tone | Soft BFP variant for editorial contexts inside BFP material — body content within a BFP article, subtle BFP-specific nav. Honey ochre emblem with ink wordmark on transparent. |
| BFB imprint | BFB-specific surfaces. Paper on slate teal. |
| BFB quiet two-tone | Soft BFB variant. Slate teal emblem with ink wordmark on transparent. |
| BFA imprint | BFA-specific surfaces. Paper on ink navy. |
| BFA quiet two-tone | Soft BFA variant. Ink navy emblem with ink wordmark on transparent. |

### Emblem variants

Eight emblem variants exist. Use the variant that matches the surface
the emblem will appear on.

| Variant | When to use |
|---|---|
| Primary positive | Default emblem on paper backgrounds. |
| Primary negative | On dark backgrounds. |
| Accent | Paper emblem on terracotta surface — high-impact small contexts. |
| Terracotta | Terracotta emblem on transparent — for use on paper-warm backgrounds where the emblem alone needs to read as branded but quiet. |
| BFP imprint | Paper emblem on honey ochre — BFP-specific small contexts. |
| BFB imprint | Paper emblem on slate teal — BFB-specific small contexts. |
| BFA imprint | Paper emblem on ink navy — BFA-specific small contexts. |
| Monochrome ink | Universal one-color fallback for the emblem. |

## Logo Usage Rules

### Clear space

The minimum clear space around any lockup is equal to the height of
the emblem within that lockup. No other graphic element, text, or
edge of a containing surface should encroach on this clear space.

The minimum clear space around any emblem is half the height of the
emblem.

### Minimum sizes

Lockups should not be reproduced smaller than 100 pixels wide
(digital) or 0.75 inches / 19mm wide (print). Below this size the
wordmark becomes difficult to read and the emblem-to-wordmark balance
breaks down. Use an emblem instead.

Emblems should not be reproduced smaller than 16 pixels square
(digital favicon size). Below this size the emblem's internal
geometry is no longer resolvable.

### What never to do with the logo

- Never apply drop shadows, glow effects, outlines, or any other
  visual effect to a lockup or emblem. The marks are designed to
  read flat.
- Never recolor the emblem and wordmark to colors other than those
  defined in the eleven lockup variants above. If a needed
  combination is not in the variant set, do not improvise — request
  an addition to this document.
- Never stretch, skew, rotate, or otherwise distort the lockup or
  emblem. Use the SVG file at the size needed; do not redraw at a
  different aspect ratio.
- Never place a lockup or emblem on a busy photograph or pattern
  that competes with the geometry of the mark. If a photographic
  background is required, ensure the lockup sits in a calm region of
  the image with sufficient contrast.
- Never type "Bitcoin-Minded" as live text and use it in place of
  the wordmark. The wordmark exists as a flattened SVG asset
  specifically so it cannot be substituted by live type that misses
  the SOFT-axis fine-tuning. Always use the SVG file.
- Never split the emblem from the wordmark and use them separately
  within the same surface, unless the design deliberately uses one
  emblem instance plus one lockup instance for distinct purposes.
- Never combine the lockup with another organization's logo to
  imply partnership unless an actual partnership exists and the
  combination has been reviewed.

## Per-Course Imprint System

Each of the three courses has a distinct visual register inside the
master identity. The imprint system is how that register is signaled
on per-course surfaces — landing pages, cohort welcomes, course
emails, course-specific documents.

### How imprints are applied

On a per-course surface, the imprint color appears in these places:

- **Hero eyebrow text** — the small uppercase label above the H1
  ("Bitcoin for People · BFP" / "Bitcoin for Business · BFB" /
  "Bitcoin for Advisors · BFA"). Imprint color, weight 500, uppercase,
  letter-spacing `0.14em`.
- **Italic emphasis words inside H1 and H2** — the italic words
  inside headlines take the imprint color rather than the master
  accent.
- **Section eyebrows** — small uppercase labels above section
  headlines. Same treatment as hero eyebrow.
- **Curriculum section numbers and left-edge stripes** — the section
  identifier (S1, S2, S3...) and a 2px vertical stripe along the
  left edge of each curriculum row.
- **Hairline above the trust meta-row** — single thin rule in
  imprint color, separating the trust row from what is above it.
- **Closing CTA top band** — a 3px imprint-color band along the
  top edge of the closing CTA section.
- **Per-course nav and footer logos** — primary positive lockup is
  the default; the per-course imprint or quiet two-tone variant may
  be substituted when course identity should dominate.

The imprint never tints page backgrounds, button fills, or body type.

### Imprint intensity by course

The three imprint colors sit at different luminosities, and the brand
applies them with different intensities accordingly.

- **BFP (honey ochre)** is the warmest and lightest of the three. It
  sits close to the master palette's warm range. BFP applies the
  imprint with a lighter touch — the section eyebrows on BFP
  surfaces stay in warm grey, not honey ochre, because honey ochre
  is close enough to the master palette that fully imprinted
  eyebrows would over-tint the page.
- **BFB (slate teal)** is colder and farther from the master palette.
  BFB applies the imprint more prominently — section eyebrows in
  slate teal, full stripe and band treatments, hairline rules in
  imprint color throughout.
- **BFA (ink navy)** is the coldest of the three and the most
  visually distant from the master palette. BFA applies the imprint
  most prominently — same treatment as BFB plus additional dominance
  through navy nav bars where appropriate to the surface.

This asymmetric application is intentional. Each imprint has its own
relationship to the master palette and is calibrated to land at the
same visible weight despite different luminosities.

## Photography Direction

*This section is a stub. It will be expanded as a body of brand
photography is produced.*

Photography across all Bitcoin-Minded surfaces uses warm natural
light, real practitioners, and matter-of-fact framing. Instructor
photography is calm, considered, and editorial — closer to a serious
magazine profile than a corporate headshot or YouTube creator
thumbnail. Avoid stock crypto imagery, neon-on-dark compositions,
abstract financial visualization, and any imagery that performs
"tech" or "finance." If a photograph cannot be produced, use no
photograph rather than a stock substitute.

This direction will be expanded with specific guidance on
composition, lighting, color grading, and approved photographers as
the brand commissions and accumulates a body of imagery.

## Maintaining This Document

Update when:

- A new course is added (new imprint color and treatment needed)
- A new logo variant is produced (asset inventory and lockup variants
  table updated)
- A color in the master palette or imprint set changes (entire color
  section reviewed for downstream impact)
- A typography decision changes (typography rules updated, related
  marketing and production documents reviewed)
- The brand direction shifts in a way that requires revisiting the
  category exclusions
- Photography direction is expanded from the current stub

Brand decisions made outside this document do not constitute updates
to the document. If a one-off decision is made for a specific surface,
either update this document to reflect the new general rule or
document the surface-specific exception in the relevant project file.
Drift accumulates silently and is the most common way brand identity
degrades.
